Full description

Institute of Aerospace Medicine

Start of employment starting by March 2027 at the latest

Neck and back pain are common among pilots, yet we know little about who develops them and why. In the new SNSF project Neck2027, we aim to develop a prognostic model that predicts the transition to clinically significant neck pain; secondary analyses address the course of neck and back pain over time. Together with the Aeromedical Institute (FAI) of the Swiss Air Force, we follow a prospective cohort of fixed-wing and helicopter pilots for 12 months, assessed during their annual aeromedical examination with biomechanical, psychological, operational and biological measures. The project is based at the Institute of Aerospace Medicine (Faculty of Medicine, University of Zurich) at Innovation Park Zurich in Dübendorf. The University of Zurich is committed to equal opportunities and explicitly welcomes applications from women.

Your responsibilities

  • You run the study from preparation to data collection: ethics submission, standardised assessments of the pilots at the FAI in Dübendorf
  • You coordinate the remote follow-ups and manage the study data in REDCap
  • You conduct the study in accordance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP)
  • Together with the project team, you prepare the data and develop the prognostic model
  • You publish the results, present them at international conferences and write your dissertation
  • You contribute to smaller related projects of our group, for example in spinal research in aviation and spaceflight, and thereby gain broad research experience

Your profile

  • Master's degree in physiotherapy or another health profession (eligible for the UZH PhD programme in Care and Rehabilitation Sciences)
  • Strong interest in aviation and space medicine
  • Excellent written and spoken English (publications, international conferences)
  • Fluent German
  • French is an advantage
  • Experience in data science and predictive modelling is an advantage
  • You enjoy working with study participants and in an interdisciplinary team, and you work reliably and independently
