Doctoral Position in Pore-Scale Modelling of Multiphase Flow and Reactive Transport

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

We are looking for a highly motivated doctoral student to develop advanced numerical models for investigating multiphase flow and reactive transport processes in porous materials. We are offering a doctoral position in the Durability of Engineering Materials research group at ETH Zurich. The group, led by Prof. Ueli Angst, addresses research challenges related to the durability of engineered materials, combining experimental and computational approaches in corrosion science, materials science, porous media, and reactive mass transport.

One important research challenge in this field is understanding multiphase transport in porous materials and its role in natural and engineered systems. As liquids move through porous materials, gas can become trapped within the pore space, forming bubbles or irregular gas clusters. These trapped gases can subsequently dissolve, grow, or change in composition, with their evolution closely linked to fluid distribution, mass transport, and chemical reactions. Understanding these processes is important for a wide range of applications, including the durability of construction materials, geological waste containment, CO2 sequestration, and underground energy storage.

Job description

  • This doctoral project aims to advance the understanding of multiphase flow and reactive transport in porous media through the development and application of advanced numerical models
  • The research will focus on the evolution of gas and liquid phases in complex pore geometries, considering processes such as gas compressibility, gas–liquid interfacial mass transfer, and (electro-)chemical reactions
  • The successful candidate will develop and apply numerical methods for pore-scale simulations of multiphase flow, coupling fluid dynamics with gas dissolution, diffusion, and reactive transport
  • The research will investigate how gas–liquid interface dynamics and pore geometry influence gas entrapment and evolution
  • The project will build on ongoing research in pore-scale modelling and offer opportunities for collaboration with experimental researchers

Profile

  • MSc degree in Mechanical Engineering, Civil Engineering, Computational Science and Engineering, Applied Mathematics, Physics, or a related field
  • Strong background in fluid mechanics and numerical methods
  • Good programming skills in Python, C++, or another scientific programming language
  • Strong analytical and problem-solving skills, with motivation to learn and conduct independent research
  • Excellent oral and written communication skills in English, and ability to work both independently and collaboratively
  • Hands-on experience in implementing numerical methods is highly desirable
  • Previous experience in computational fluid dynamics (CFD) is desirable, while experience in multiphase flow modelling is an additional advantage
  • Above all, candidates should be curious and enthusiastic about scientific research
