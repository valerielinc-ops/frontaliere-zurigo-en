Doctoral Position in Social Networks in Education

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

We are an interdisciplinary lab studying social and behavioral phenomena using network concepts. Members of the lab actively develop theories, methods and software in a variety of domains, for our own empirical studies and for our collaborative endeavors across multiple discplines. The group of Prof. Christoph Stadtfeld is looking for a motivated Doctoral student interested in the field of Social Networks in Education .This position is fully funded and a joint degree with EPFL Lausanne.

Project background

The position is embedded in the Joint Doctoral Program in Learning Science (JDPLS) of ETH Zurich and EPFL Lausanne. The Social Networks Lab takes a sociological perspective on learning, understanding, and education within the JDPLS. One core focus of our lab is the study of social networks in education, aiming to understand how students at various levels of education form social relationships with their peers, how those aggregate into complex, dynamic social networks, and how individuals’ relationships and their network positions affect very personal outcomes, such as their well-being, motivation, learning processes, and academic success. With whom students form social ties can largely depend on institutional decisions, for example, about group and lecture assignments. The most visible example of this research line is the Swiss StudentLife Study which was conducted from 2016 to 2026. It investigated undergraduate students' social and academic intregration over the course of several years. Social networks research in educational contexts is highly interdisciplinary and simultaneously addresses theoretical, statistical, and research design challenges. Members of the lab combine research approaches from the social sciences, statistics, and computer science. They address empirical research questions with modern ways of data collection (often combining data from digital behavioral traces with surveys) and advance the development of statistical methods, algorithms, and scientific software. The SN-LAB thereby offers a sociological perspective to research questions in education and learning science.

Job description

During this project, you will be at the forefront of social network research, both independently and as a member of our research team. Your responsibilities may encompass:

  • Developing theoretical and empirical perspectives on the social embeddedness of learning, understanding, and education
  • Conceptualizing and designing innovative observational research designs, data collection strategies and behavioral experiments in educational settings
  • Analyzing network data with cutting-edge statistical and computational methods
  • Engaging in interdisciplinary dialogues (e.g., learning science, sociology of education, statistics, computer science, computational social science, etc.) to enhance our knowledge how social networks emerge and change in educational settings, and how they affect the individuals they connect
  • Presenting research progress at related conferences to an international, multidisciplinary audience

Profile

Successful candidates should have the following qualifications:

  • An excellent Master's degree in learning science or quantitative behavioral sciences with a focus on learning and education (e.g., learning science, sociology of education, applied math, social data science, psychology)
  • Prior work such as a Master thesis that demonstrates a strong interest in learning science and quantitative social network research
  • Excellent quantitative analysis and data management skills, including experience in or willingness to learn R or Python
  • Strong skills in scientific writing
  • Ability and willingness to work productively in an interdisciplinary, international team

We offer an inspiring research environment at one of the world's top universities, close collaboration in a small research lab that is part of a leading international network of social networks researchers, advanced training in modern social networks research, generous research funding and a competitive salary.
