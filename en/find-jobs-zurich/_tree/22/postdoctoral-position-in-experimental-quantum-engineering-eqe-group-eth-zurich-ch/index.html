Postdoctoral Position in Experimental Quantum Engineering (EQE) Group

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

The Experimental Quantum Engineering group at ETH Zurich and the Paul Scherrer Institute (PSI), led by Professor Wenchao Xu , invites applications for a Postdoctoral Researcher position in experimental quantum science with dual-species atomic systems and Rydberg interactions.

We are looking for a highly motivated experimental physicist who enjoys building advanced quantum experiments, taking scientific and technical ownership of an apparatus, and exploring new research directions at the interface of atomic physics, quantum simulation, quantum optics, and quantum information science.

The successful candidate will take a leading role in the development and operation of an experimental apparatus dedicated to exploring novel approaches to quantum information processing and quantum many-body physics. The apparatus supports two complementary research directions: (i) quantum interconnects based on Rydberg-mediated light–matter interactions, and (ii) developing novel measurement protocols that use a small number of local quantum probes to extract information about complex quantum many-body systems. Both directions offer exciting opportunities to develop new experimental capabilities and pursue independent scientific questions.

Project background

The project is based on dual-species Rydberg systems developed within our group, with which the group has successfully demonstrated generation and detection of dual-species Rydberg systems, as well as the precise control over interspeices Rydberg interactions. They share the broader capabilities of addressing distinct scientific questions, and provide a unique opportunity for the successful candidate to take scientific and technical leadership of a versatile experimental platform while exploring two complementary frontiers of quantum science.

  1. Rydberg-mediated quantum interconnects

A central challenge in scaling quantum processors is establishing fast and high-fidelity connections between spatially separated quantum systems. Photons provide natural carriers of quantum information over long distances, but efficiently interfacing stationary qubits with propagating photons remains a major experimental challenge.

This project explores a novel approach to quantum interconnects based on strong Rydberg interactions between individual atoms and mesoscopic atomic ensembles. Building on our recent theoretical proposal, Fast Quantum Interconnects via Neutral Atom Ensembles (arXiv:2608.05147), the approach exploits collective light–matter coupling and Rydberg-mediated interactions to generate entanglement between stationary atomic qubits and propagating photons, without requiring optical cavities. The proposed architecture offers the potential for fast entanglement generation and scalable connections between neutral-atom quantum processors.

  1. Learning and sensing complex quantum many-body systems with local quantum probes

Understanding complex quantum many-body systems is a central challenge in modern physics. In many situations, however, accessing the full quantum state or directly measuring all microscopic degrees of freedom is practically impossible. An exciting alternative is to use a small number of local quantum probes to extract information about an otherwise inaccessible many-body environment.

This project explores new approaches to quantum-enabled characterization and sensing of complex many-body systems. Rather than directly manipulating the entire many-body system, we investigate how information about its microscopic properties, collective dynamics, and correlations can be inferred through the interactions between the system and a limited number of local quantum probes. The research programme spans different scales of quantum characterization, ranging from microscopic Hamiltonian learning, through probing mesoscopic many-body dynamics, to characterizing macroscopic quantum properties and exploring quantum-enhanced sensing.

Job description

The successful candidate will take scientific and technical leadership of the experimental apparatus and play a central role in establishing and advancing the two research directions.

Responsibilities include

• Experimental apparatus leadership: Lead the development and operation of the experimental apparatus, including laser systems, optical setups and experimental control • Research on quantum interconnects: Develop and implement Rydberg-mediated light–matter interfaces, investigate collective atom–photon interactions, and pursue experimental demonstrations of photon-mediated entanglement generation • Research on learning quantum many-body physics through local probes: Develop experimental capabilities for probing complex quantum systems with local quantum probes, and investigate Hamiltonian learning, many-body dynamics, and quantum-enhanced sensing • Experimental innovation and scientific leadership: Identify and develop new experimental techniques and capabilities to advance the scientific objectives of both research directions. Coordinate experimental activities across the two research programmes • Supervision and collaboration: Mentor and supervise Doctoral students and master students, and contribute to scientific collaborations within and outside the research group
