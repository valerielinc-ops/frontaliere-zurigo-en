SBB CFF FFS You can look forward to that. You actively shape the reliability of our trains and help our customers arrive safely and on time. You can expect a collegial environment in which knowledge is shared and you can take on responsibility. At the same time, you have the opportunity to further develop your expertise and continuously expand your know-how in modern diagnostic systems. You see irregular working hours and shift work as an advantage to enjoy your free time while others work. Night and weekend work is also paid for, for example, an hourly wage supplement of 16 francs for Sunday work.   • As a diagnostician: in passenger transport, you get to the bottom of disruptions on trains. With modern diagnostic tools, you ensure that our vehicles are safe and reliable on the road.
  • Whether in ongoing operation or during planned downtimes: You recognize causes, make well-founded diagnoses and initiate targeted measures.
  • When it comes down to it, you’re there: you fix disruptions quickly and sustainably.
  • Together with the team, you work on projects to increase vehicle availability and reduce downtime.
  • Beyond the diagnosis, you also get involved and support the team in further tasks at the location.

You bring that with you.

  • Team spirit, clear communication and analytical thinking set you apart. You also like to solve complex problems independently.
  • Irregular working hours, including night and weekend work, give you the opportunity to make your free time flexible. For this function, you need a category B driver’s license.
  • Very good knowledge of German (level C1) in word and writing you bring with you. Knowledge of a second national language is an advantage.
  • You have completed a vocational apprenticeship in technical or electrical direction – for example as an automotive mechatronics engineer:in, production mechanic:in, polymechanic:in or electrical installer:in and bring appropriate professional practice.
