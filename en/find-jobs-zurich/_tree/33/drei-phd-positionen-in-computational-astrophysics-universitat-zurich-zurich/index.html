Full description

Department of Astrophysics

Start of employment from 1 January 2027, temporary

The Department of Astrophysics at the University of Zurich invites applications for three PhD positions in computational astrophysics. The positions are part of the Swiss National Science Foundation project “Euclid Science in Switzerland” and are funded for up to four years. ESA's Euclid space telescope is mapping billions of galaxies across a third of the sky to uncover the nature of dark matter and dark energy. Interpreting these data relies on simulations and theoretical predictions of matching precision, and our group provides them: we develop the N-body code PKDGRAV3 and produced the Euclid Flagship simulations, among the largest cosmological simulations ever performed. The successful candidates will join this effort and work within the international Euclid Consortium, in close collaboration with partners at ETH Zurich, EPFL, FHNW and the University of Geneva. They will be supervised by Dr. Douglas Potter, Prof. Dr. Joachim Stadel and Dr. Aurel Schneider. The scientific computing infrastructure at the University of Zurich is excellent and includes dedicated access to the facilities of the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre (CSCS). Zurich offers a dynamic and international living environment and is consistently ranked among the world's best cities for quality of living.

Your responsibilities

The research covers three broad, overlapping areas

  • Flagship simulations: Develop and run the next-generation Euclid Flagship simulation with PKDGRAV3 on GPU-accelerated supercomputers. This includes extending the code to identify dark matter halos and their substructure on the fly, and working with our partners to turn the simulation into mock galaxy catalogues for Euclid.
  • Emulators: Build the next generation of the Euclid Emulator: machine-learning models, trained on large suites of simulations, that predict cosmological statistics in milliseconds. The goal is to extend the emulator from the matter distribution to galaxy clustering and lensing statistics.
  • Baryonification and multi-probe cosmology: Model how gas and stars alter the matter distribution predicted by N-body simulations, and develop a pipeline that produces consistent synthetic sky maps (weak lensing, galaxy clustering, X-ray and Sunyaev–Zel'dovich) for Euclid and complementary surveys.

This is a unique opportunity to work at the intersection of astrophysics and advanced computational methods.

Your profile

Applicants are expected to hold a master's degree (or equivalent) in physics, astronomy or a related field by the starting date. Candidates with a strong interest in high-performance computing, machine learning or numerical astrophysics are especially encouraged to apply. Programming experience is an asset.
