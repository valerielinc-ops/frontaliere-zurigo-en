Doctoral position in cognitive and moral heuristics in lay people’s reactions to climate change adaptation

100%, Zurich, fixed-term

We are looking for a motivated doctoral student who is interested in applying psychological concepts and methodologies to environmental topics. The position is available starting January 2027.

Project background

While climate change mitigation (e.g., reduction of greenhouse gas emissions) remains important, it is also clear that societies need to adapt to the unavoidable hazards associated with climate change. The recent heat waves in Europe and the health and economical damages they caused, exemplify that adaptation measures are not yet sufficiently in place.

Heuristics (mental shortcuts) can bias people’s risk perceptions and affect moral considerations. As such they can influence people’s private adaptation behaviors and their perceptions of technologies that could help societies to deal better with the consequences of climate change. By influencing regulations, they can also impact the exposure of vulnerable groups to hazards, such as heat.

The aim of this doctoral thesis is to gain insights in how cognitive and moral heuristics influence the effective implementation of climate change adaptation measures.
