Department of Quantitative Biomedicine

Start of employment immediately

The Department of Quantitative Biomedicine combines biomedical research, with biotechnology, and computational biology to develop the foundations of next generation precision medicine. Within this environment, we are a research group aiming to understand the pathogenic mechanisms leading to two major neurodegenerative diseases: amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD). Polymenidou Lab

Your responsibilities

We are looking for a technician who supports human (stem) cell culture and lab administration. The technician will support the day-to-day operation of our research laboratory, contributing to routine laboratory activities and administrative tasks related to our research on human neurodegenerative diseases. The laboratory uses established cell lines, human stem cell cultures (iPSCs, NSCs), and iNets-our unique model of human neural networks (Hruska-Plochan et al., Nature, 2024). The position will involve a range of routine laboratory activities, including supporting neural culture work and performing tasks such as aliquoting, medium preparation, and other basic laboratory procedures. Some work with established cell cultures will also be required, including routine handling, expansion, and banking of existing iPSC and NSC lines. The role also includes laboratory administration and management tasks, such as ordering supplies, managing inventory, receiving and organizing materials, and supporting general laboratory organization. The position is particularly suited to someone who enjoys working in an academic research laboratory, is comfortable with structured and repetitive routine tasks, and takes pride in keeping laboratory activities organized and running smoothly. The successful candidate will have experience with molecular biology techniques and mammalian cell culture, including human cell lines. Experience with human iPSCs and/or neurons is not required but would be advantageous. Additionally, experience with the following techniques would be an asset, but is not required:

  • Long-term cell culture
  • Immunofluorescence (IF) staining of fixed cells
  • Basic handling of light microscopes
  • Basic knowledge of cell and developmental biology
