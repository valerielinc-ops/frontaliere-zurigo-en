PhD position in the field of nonlinear vibrational metamaterials

The Laboratory for Acoustics/Noise Control is an international team of scientists and engineers active in the fields of vibroacoustics, environmental acoustics, and psychoacoustics. The diverse research topics rely on extensive numerical models and a broad range of experimental equipment for noise and vibration measurements.

Mechanical waves and vibrations are traditionally controlled using linear devices with fixed properties. In this PhD project, the student will explore a new generation of nonlinear resonators whose dynamic behaviour changes with vibration amplitude, enabling functionalities that are not achievable with conventional designs. The research will combine mechanical modelling, finite-element simulations, prototype development, and experimental testing. The goal is to develop efficient models and design rules for nonlinear metamaterials capable of adaptive vibration isolation, frequency conversion, and non-reciprocal wave propagation. The successful candidate will work within a collaborative Swiss-French project involving Empa, ETH Zurich, and LAUM.

Your tasks

As doctoral candidate within this project you will work on highly nonlinear building blocks for vibrational metamaterials. Specific tasks to achieve the challenging results include

  • Develop analytical and numerical models for strongly nonlinear resonators exhibiting nonlinear stiffness, damping, and inertia effects.
  • Design, simulate, and optimize novel resonator concepts based on inertia amplification, bilinear stiffness, and multi-degree-of-freedom dynamics.
  • Manufacture and experimentally characterize resonator prototypes using vibration testing, laser vibrometry, and modal analysis techniques.
  • Investigate nonlinear phenomena such as internal resonances, frequency conversion, bifurcations, and nonlinear energy transfer.
  • Create reduced-order models and efficient simulation tools for the design of nonlinear metamaterials and wave-control devices.
  • Disseminate research results through publications in leading journals, presentations at international conferences, and collaboration with project partners at ETH Zurich and the Université du Mans.
